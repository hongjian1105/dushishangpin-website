BRUCE LEE

Bruce Lee

Explore products & design

Beyond strength. An enduring philosophy.

Bruce Lee (1940–1973) left an influence spanning martial arts, cinema and popular culture. From his upbringing in Hong Kong to teaching in the United States and his breakthroughs on screen, he continually sought an authentic form of self-expression.

Beyond physical training, Lee read, wrote and explored the relationship between movement and the camera. The openness and personal expression at the heart of Jeet Kune Do carried his influence beyond cinema. Photographs, teaching records and published work reveal a life of learning, reflection and creativity.

  1. 1940

    Born in San Francisco and raised in Hong Kong.

  2. 1959–1960s

    Moved to the United States, taught martial arts and developed his own approach.

  3. 1971—1973

    Returned to Hong Kong and made films with a lasting international impact.

JEWEL / CREATED BY STAN LEE

Jewel

Explore products & design

From a real-life inspiration to a heroic imagination.

Jewel is a personal character created by Stan Lee for Hong Kong singer G.E.M. Her voice and stage presence became important inspirations for the character.

Jewel was publicly introduced at a Stan Lee-related event in Shanghai in 2018. The original artwork combines a distinctive silhouette, stage atmosphere and dynamic movement, translating a musician’s personal expression into a heroic character. Explore the origins first, then discover product designs and later creative interpretations.

  1. The inspiration

    Stan Lee met G.E.M.; her personality and voice inspired the character.

  2. 2018

    Jewel was publicly introduced.

  3. Creative extensions

    From the original character to product design and visual storytelling.

STAN LEE / CREATIVE LEGACY

Stan Lee

Explore products & design

Every hero begins with an idea.

Stan Lee (1922–2018) was a comic-book writer, editor and creator. Working with artists including Jack Kirby and Steve Ditko, he brought everyday concerns, emotions and responsibility into superhero stories.

From story ideas and notes on comic panels to dialogue and the wider world of moving images, creativity ran throughout his life. Photographs, comic notes and public appearances reflect his enduring enthusiasm for stories, readers and imagination.

  1. 1941

    Published early prose under the name Stan Lee.

  2. 1960s

    Collaborated with artists to shape enduring comic-book characters.

  3. 2008

    Awarded the National Medal of Arts; his creative legacy continues today.
